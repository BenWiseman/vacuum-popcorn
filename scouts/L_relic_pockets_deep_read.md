## 1. Khater, Marsh, Nikolis, 2609.24714 (21 Sep 2026, JCAP)

**Model and trapping.** No potential here; Sec. 2 follows ref. [5], the only earlier ARP paper: Carenza, Eby, Iarygina, Marsh, JHEP 09 (2024) 023, arXiv:2407.01676. The setting is "axions trapped in spherical false vacuum regions, separated by a dilaton phase boundary from the surrounding true vacuum", and across the boundary "the axion mass increases rapidly" (Sec. 2.1). No values for m_a|FV or m_a|TV; m_a|FV drops out because "the axions inside the pocket are ultra-relativistic". No trapping inequality here.

**Stability.** "The equilibrium radius of a stabilised pocket is set by pressure balance and energy conservation" (Eq. 2.2). N_a must stay large, which "puts an upper limit on the transition temperature, T̃_t ≲ 10^10" (T̃_t = T_t/1 TeV).

**Benchmarks.** T_t ≳ 0.5·10⁴ TeV gives "R_pocket ≲ 1.2·10⁻¹¹ m" and gas "temperature of T ≳ 2.4 TeV" (Sec. 1). Sec. 2.1: "The masses of the pockets in this parameter space range from 28.5 kg (for T̃_t = 0.5·10⁴) to 2·10⁹ GeV (for T̃_t = 10^10), and contain axions with energies E_a ≃ 7.3 TeV (for T̃_t = 0.5·10⁴) to E_a ≃ 0.4 EeV (for T̃_t = 10^10)." These are the thermal gas energy E_a ≃ 3T_a = 3·3.65 GeV (g̃⋆/ϵ)^{1/4} T̃_t^{3/4} (2.7) at the two ends, in the pocket frame. (mine) Eq. 2.7 gives 7.33 TeV and 0.390 EeV; Eq. 2.6 gives N_a ≈ 4 at the top; 10²⁰ eV needs T_t ≈ 1.6·10¹⁶ GeV, above their 10¹³ GeV limit.

**Collapse of small pockets.** Sec. 2.2, for R_∗ ≲ 1/T_SM: "the number of axions in such small pockets is O(1), and classically, one would expect such regions to collapse. Two effects can alter this conclusion: first, consistently characterising such small pockets calls for a full quantum treatment; second, the high curvature of the pockets walls leads to non-thermal axion production inside the pockets, which may contribute to stabilising the pockets. We postpone a detailed investigation of these possibilities for future work." Other deferrals (Secs. 1, 2, 2.1, 2.2, 3.3, 7) do not concern collapse. NOT FOUND: collapse of a pocket today; particles leaving a pocket.

**Signals.** Inverse Primakoff in atomic fields; for k_aR ≫ 1, Γ = g²_{aγγ}E_0²R_pocket/3 (3.48), "independent of energy"; photon energy equals E_a. Sec. 5: "A ‘smoking gun’ signal of ARP dark matter is upward-going electromagnetic showers uniformly distributed over the detector volume." "the ARP theories considered in this paper cannot explain the ‘anomalous’ upward-going ANITA events" (Sec. 6.1.1).

**Dark matter.** All of it by construction (2.1). n_pocket = 1/D³ (App. B.1); D_pocket in (2.5).

## 2. Carenza et al., 2407.01676

Abstract: pockets are "regions of relic false vacua stabilised by the pressure from a kinematically trapped, hot axion gas".

**Potential and masses.** V_eff(φ,a) = M⁴ e^{−8π²φ/Λ} (1 − cos(a/f_a)) (2.2), φ = Λ/g² the dilaton; V_tot(φ) unspecified, φ_FV > φ_TV. m_a²|_TV = m_a²|_FV exp[S_FV |Δφ|/φ_FV] (4.2). Inside, m_a|_FV ≳ 3H(t_t) for misalignment (Sec. 4).

**Trapping.** "Reflective boundary conditions are guaranteed for axions with a total energy less than" m_a|_TV, and "provide an excellent approximation as long as" T_a ≲ O(10 m_a|_TV) (Sec. 4). Sec. 9: "According to [23], the trapping is efficient when" m_a|_TV ≳ O(10 T_eq). Ref. [23] is Lewicki et al.

**Dynamics (Sec. 5).** "Shortly before the wall would collapse, the front ‘passes through the origin’ and strikes the contracting dilaton wall, causing it to bounce back with a high γ_w factor." "Our simulations remain stable over multiple cycles", with no damping timescale. Equilibrium: R_eq = 2σ/(P − ΔV) (5.1).

**Escape.** NOT FOUND: escaping energies or spectra. Nearest, for thick walls: "it becomes possible for axions to partly penetrate the wall before being reflected" (Sec. 9). Sec. 1: collapsing relic pockets were proposed "as seeds of primordial black holes (PBHs); however, it was subsequently shown that the presence of trapped relativistic particles decreases the compactness, making it more difficult for PBHs to form" (refs. 2105.07481, 2106.00111; then 2205.05667, 2305.04924, 2305.07702).

## 3. Lewicki et al., 2305.07702

**Rule (Eqs. 1, 2).** Wall frame; + is true vacuum, − false; particle from region i to j: p^μ → p^μ + n^μ n·p F_i(−n·p), F_i(u) = 2 for u² < m_j² − m_i², else 1 − √(1 + (m_i² − m_j²)/u²). Here u is the wall-frame normal momentum (mine).

**Energy and escape.** "At the minimal radius, when the maximal compactness is reached, the particles carry almost all of the energy". "collisions with a nearly relativistic wall can easily accelerate the particles to relativistic speeds and provide sufficient energy for them to escape." NOT FOUND: formula, plot or spectrum of escaping energy; only fluxes through the wall for m_− = 0 (A5).

**Criteria.** Sec. II, after Eq. 6: "R → 0 can be realized only if all particles eventually escape the bubble and the final stages of the collapse proceed as they would in vacuum." A terminal wall speed exists if ΔV/ρ_init ≳ Δm²/(12T_−²) (11). "For large ΔV/ρ_init there is no initial terminal velocity for the wall and the bubble collapses within time t ≈ R_0". At v_w = −0.6, "Δm/T_− ≳ 10 is sufficient to avoid runaway collapse during the simulation". Criteria are energy ratios, not particle counts; full collapse needs M > N m_+. App. A: "Walls with Lorentz factors above the black line are unstable since a small upward fluctuation in velocity would decrease the pressure", so the wall runs to γ → ∞. The thin-wall approximation works well "until the bubble's size becomes comparable to the thickness of the wall".

## 4. Kawana, Lu, Xie, 2206.09923, Sec. 2.2

"Consider a spherical shrinking FV remnant. In the vicinity of the bubble wall, we can effectively treat it as a plane moving towards the" −z direction. Elastic flip p′_z → −p′_z (2.7). Ẽ = [(1+v_w²)E + 2v_w p_z]/(1−v_w²) (2.8); δE = 2v_w(v_w E + p_z)/(1−v_w²) (2.9), "as expected, assuming the particles remain trapped." Massless thermal particles (2.14), local equilibrium (Sec. 3). Escape after crossing is not computed; App. B (B.1) gives only the thermal tail above the gap. Sec. 4.1: "particle trapping may become inefficient [71, 72] because the wall velocity becomes ultra-relativistic, which results in a large boosted energy of particles in the wall frame" (refs. 1912.02830, 1912.04238).

## 5. Late Fermi-ball collapse

**2210.16462.** The Fermi ball "rapidly transitions to a PBH [41]. In our scenario, this final transition can happen after recombination". The fermions end inside the hole. Before that, partly trapped fermions leak: "the high energy tail of the Fermi-Dirac distribution may slowly escape and cool the remnant" (Eq. 28). NOT FOUND: emission or energy at the final collapse. Timing: a transition "between the CMB era and the present day" needs 20 ≲ M_χ/T_{D,1} ≲ 40, and "For even larger mass to temperature ratios, there is the interesting possibility of producing PBH from collapsing thermal balls in the future." Today is inside the range, not a stated target.

**2305.13429.** "If the timescale of collapse is slow for these structures, it is possible to produce black holes extremely late in the universe, even into the future [16, 18]." The population "can form even today". After collapse, emission is Hawking only: "producing large fluxes of photons and other particles". NOT FOUND: ejection of trapped fermions at collapse.

## (mine) Does Lewicki Eq. 2 give m_out²/m_in?

A particle at rest inside, hit head-on by a wall of Lorentz factor g, crosses only if g ≥ m_out/m_in. Its lab energy after crossing is g(g m_in − v√(g²m_in² − m_out²)): g m_out ≈ m_out²/m_in at threshold, tending to (m_in² + m_out²)/(2m_in) for g ≫ m_out/m_in. Checked numerically.
