# Famous UHECR events: primary type tests and exposures

Sources read as PDF text (arXiv) or INSPIRE abstract records. NOT FOUND / UNREAD marks anything I did not open.

## A. Primary-type results

**A1. Fly's Eye 320 EeV event (15 Oct 1991)**
- Bird et al. 1995 (astro-ph/9410067): "It might even have been a gamma ray." and "The longitudinal profile of the Fly's Eye shower does not identify the primary particle type." Photon would "reach maximum size earlier" if it pre-showers in the geomagnetic field (arrival "nearly perpendicular to the local geomagnetic field").
- Risse et al. 2004, "On the primary particle type of the most energetic Fly's Eye event" (astro-ph/0410739, a conference proceeding, not the Astropart. Phys. paper). Lead confirmed. Xmax = 815 +- 60 g/cm2. Photon: P = 13 %, 1.5 sigma discrepancy. p, C, Fe: P = 43/54/53 % (QGSJET 01), 31/52/54 % (SIBYLL 2.1), 0.6-1.0 sigma. Quote: "Both primary photons and any hadron between proton and iron nuclei can not be excluded as primary particle of the 320 EeV Fly's Eye event." With an extreme photonuclear cross-section the photon discrepancy drops from 1.5 to "about 1.2 sigma". Their full-length paper is Astropart. Phys. 21 (2004) 479 (cited there; not opened).
- Halzen, Vazquez, Stanev, Vankov, Astropart. Phys. 3, 151 (1995): **LEAD WRONG on the arXiv number.** astro-ph/9410069 is Elbert and Sommers, "In Search of a Source for the 320 EeV Fly's Eye Cosmic Ray". INSPIRE record 374697 lists no arXiv eprint. INSPIRE abstract (Elsevier source): "We show that the event is not initiated by a photon and that the assignments of a proton and neutrino as the primary particle are, arguably, equally valid." Full text UNREAD. So 1995: photon excluded (Halzen et al.). 2004: photon allowed at 1.5 sigma (Risse et al.). The two disagree.

**A2. AGASA above 1e20 eV**
- Count: 11 events above 1e20 eV, muon density measured in 6 (Risse 2005, astro-ph/0502418: "From eleven AGASA showers reconstructed with energies above 100 EeV, the muon content in the shower is measured in six").
- Risse et al. 2005 (astro-ph/0502418, PRL 95, 171102; lead confirmed): "upper limit on the photon fraction of 51% (67%) at a confidence level of 90% (95%) for primary energies above 1.25 x 10^20 eV". Uses 6 events. Stable range on dropping one event: "61-78% (95% CL)". Photon limit rises to 75 % with another cross-section extrapolation (sigma_mod), and "no upper limit could be set with high confidence" with the extreme one.
- Shinozaki et al. 2002 (ApJ 571, L117; INSPIRE 590029, no arXiv number found; abstract only): "upper limits on the gamma-ray flux are estimated to be 28% above 10^19 eV and 67% above 10^19.5 eV ... at a confidence level of 95%. Above 10^20 eV, ... there is no indication that the observed events are mostly gamma-ray showers." **LEAD CORRECTION:** the thresholds are 1e19 and 10^19.5 eV, not an above-1e20 limit.
- Rubtsov et al. 2006 (astro-ph/0601449, PRD 73, 063009; lead confirmed): "case-by-case ten highest energy events with known muon content and conclude that at the 95% confidence level none of them was induced by a primary photon", and limit "36% at 95% C.L. on the fraction of primary photons ... above 10^20 eV". 10 events = 6 AGASA + 4 Yakutsk (one Yakutsk event at 0.85e20). Table I: the 3 Dec 1993 AGASA event (E = 2.13e20 eV) has P(photon, E>1e20) = 0.001. The 2.46e20 eV AGASA event (10 May 2001, 3 muon detectors) has 0.000. Glushkov 2007 (astro-ph/0701245) says of the Risse AGASA limit: "problems in accounting for the difference between actual and reconstructed photon energies in that work".

**A3. Yakutsk**
- Rubtsov 2006 Table I: 7 May 1989 event, E = 1.50e20 (hadronic scale), zenith 58.7 deg, 5 muon detectors, rho_mu(1000) = 11.8 m^-2, P(photon, E>1e20) = 0.000. Yakutsk "observed three events above 10^20 eV, all with measured muon content" (zenith < 60 deg).
- Glushkov et al. 2007 (astro-ph/0701245): limits are 22 % for E0 > 4e19 eV and 12 % for E0 > 2e19 eV (95 % CL). No limit above 1e20. **LEAD CORRECTION:** it does not treat the 1989 event separately.
- Glushkov et al. 2025 (arXiv:2506.00517): treats two giant showers (7 May 1989, log E = 20.05 in Table 1, text "The modern estimation is E ~ 1.1x10^20 eV"; and 2 Apr 2024, log E = 19.81). The word "photon" does not appear in the text. Quote: "abnormally high fraction of muons, which is beyond current model predictions ... there is also a possibility that these showers were initiated by some exotic primary particles." Muon/SD ratio at 600 m is 1.17 and 0.8 against 0.83 and 0.62 expected for iron. Photon showers are muon-poor, so this implicitly disfavours a photon, but no test is printed.

**A4. Pierre Auger SD photon search (2209.05926, JCAP 05 (2023) 021)**
- Quote: "We find 16 (1) [0] photon candidates above 10^19 eV (2x10^19 eV) [4x10^19 eV]." **LEAD CORRECTION:** thresholds are 10, 20, 40 EeV, with 16, 1, 0 candidates. Consistency: "in statistical agreement with what is expected from the exponential fit to the burn sample, with a difference of -0.3 standard deviations". Search sample 48,061 events (after the burn sample). No candidates coincident in time, no small-scale clustering.
- Exposure: "A = 46,900 km2 sr yr the total exposure in the time period 1 January 2004 to 30 June 2020", zenith 30-60 deg.
- Limits (95 % CL, E^-2): 2.11e-3, 3.12e-4, 1.72e-4 km^-2 sr^-1 yr^-1 above 10, 20, 40 EeV.
- Above 1e20 eV: nothing printed. Search is restricted to E_gamma > 1e19 eV. The paper only says "the detection of photons of even higher energies, above 10^20 eV, would open an unexpected window". No Auger event above 1e20 eV is tagged as a photon candidate in anything I read.

**A5. Telescope Array**
- 2512.01638 (v2, 23 Apr 2026; neural net on TA SD, data 2008-05-11 to 2022-05-10): Table 2 gives limits and observed photon candidates.
  - 10^19.0 eV: 3 candidates, Phi < 2.3e-3
  - 10^19.5 eV: 1 candidate (expected background 0.18), Phi < 8.2e-4
  - 10^20.0 eV: **0 candidates** (expected background 0.06), Phi < 3.0e-4 km^-2 sr^-1 yr^-1, photon fraction < 0.21
  - Abstract: "Phi_gamma(E_gamma > 10^19 eV) < 2.3e-3, and Phi_gamma(E_gamma > 10^20 eV) < 3.0e-4". The 1e19.5 limit is not in the abstract.
  - Effective exposure (Table 1, 10^3 km2 sr yr): 0.98 (18.5), 3.58 (19.0), 6.30 (19.5), 10.44 (20.0).
  - The number of TA events above 1e20 eV in this data set is NOT printed in this paper.
- 2311.14231 (Science, 244 EeV event; confirmed): the classifier "excludes a photon as the primary particle at the 99.986% confidence level, instead favoring a primary proton". It "cannot distinguish between protons and heavier nuclei". Also: "28 TA SD events with energy >100 EeV observed between 2008 May and 2021 November ... The total exposure is 1.6 x 10^4 km2 sr yr."

**A6. Others.** Volcano Ranch (Linsley 1962), Haverah Park, SUGAR, HiRes highest event: UNREAD. Only secondary mention: Risse 2005 cites Haverah Park limits "48% above 10 EeV and 50% above 40 EeV (95% CL)", which are not above 1e20.

## B. Exposures (as printed)

1. Auger. ApJ 935, 170 (2206.13492): "integrated exposure of 122,000 km2 sr yr" above 32 EeV, 1 Jan 2004 to 31 Dec 2020. Parts: "95,700 km2 sr yr for the vertical sample and 26,300 km2 sr yr for the inclined" (2,040 + 595 events above 32 EeV). Phase I catalog (2211.16020): no km2 sr yr figure printed. It lists the 100 highest-energy events, 78 to 166 EeV, 1 Jan 2004 to 31 Dec 2020; the sky map colour bar shows directional exposure to 20,000 km2 yr. Number above 100 EeV: not located.
2. Telescope Array. 2311.14231: 28 events above 100 EeV, 2008 May to 2021 Nov (13.5 yr), 1.6e4 km2 sr yr. 2406.19287 (14 yr, 2008-05-11 to 2022-05-10, zenith < 55 deg): 5978 events above 10 EeV, "including the event with the highest energy of 244 EeV and 18 other events with E > 100 EeV", so 19 above 100 EeV. No exposure figure printed. The 28 and 19 differ because of event selection (zenith cut and period); I did not check which.
3. AGASA (astro-ph/0209422, Astropart. Phys. 19, 447; lead confirmed, title "Energy determination in the Akeno Giant Air Shower Array experiment"): "exposure ... almost constant at 5.1 x 10^16 m2 s sr above 10^19 eV" for zenith < 45 deg, data up to July 2002. My conversion: about 1.6e3 km2 sr yr. Array area is about 100 km2. The same paper says "The present highest energy event may only be limited by exposure."
4. Fly's Eye. Bird 1995: no exposure at 3e20 eV found by text search (aperture, exposure, km). NOT FOUND.

## C. Photon-to-nucleon ratio, heavy-particle decay

- Aloisio, Berezinsky, Kachelriess 2004 (hep-ph/0307279; lead confirmed): "At x ~ 1 x 10^-3 this ratio [gamma/N, generation spectrum, M_X = 1e14 GeV] is characterized by a value of 2 - 3 only." With x = E/M_X, x = 1e-3 is E = 1e20 eV. Fig. 18 gives the band and the paper states DGLAP gives "very similar results". The decrease is "caused by a decrease of the number of pions in the new calculations and by an increase of the number of nucleons". Photons exceed 50 % of the flux "at E > (7-8) x 10^19 eV". Conclusions: "In present calculations this excess diminishes to gamma/N ~ 2-3".
- At Earth, Galactic halo with added extragalactic protons, at about 1e20 eV: "the ratio gamma/p predicted by the SHDM model is 1.2 - 1.4". It compares this to AGASA's "gamma/p <= 2 ... at E = 3 x 10^19 eV". Photon fraction at Earth is not printed.
- Auger 2208.02353: "the presence of 2-to-3 (3-to-4) times more photons (neutrinos) than nucleons". Auger 2203.08854 and Das, Murase, Fujii 2302.02993 (uses HDMSpectra): no gamma/N ratio printed in what I grepped.
- Berezinsky, Kachelriess, Vilenkin 1997: UNREAD.
